get their products from the seller or warehouse to the customers and back via

How Does eCommerce Logistics Work?
• eCommerce logistics refers to the entire series of processes, from receiving an
online order to the safe delivery of the package to the customer by the
estimated date and time. The 2 major processes involved in eCommerce
Logistics are:
1. Forward Logistics
2. Reverse Logistics
<!-- p5 -->
Forward Logistics
• Inventory Management
This process is highly critical to keeping regular track of inventory. While stacking
products in the warehouse or fulfillment center, warehouse management should occur
where the high-demand products must be kept handy or easily reachable, followed by the
goods where their accessibility is in proportion to their demand.
eCommerce Logistics companies need to put in measures to ensure the safety and
security of the inventory in their warehouses or storage spaces.
• Smart Inventory Placement
Smart inventory placement refers to the automated recommendation of the best storage
facility to place your inventory at based on a variety of factors specific to each SKU, such
as seasonal demand, proximity to areas of high demand, market trends and more.
This allows your inventory to be fulfilled faster and more efficiently every time an order is
placed.
<!-- p6 -->
Forward Logistics
• Order Preparation
The next step is to gather the inventory and prepare the final order per the customer’s
request. The next step is packaging and labelling. Products are packaged in a single SKU
or a kit with several SKUs before being labelled as per their batches and destinations.
Many companies have adopted eco-friendly packaging and labelling to lure more aware
customers.
• Shipping Processes
Safe and timely delivery of orders is critical to the overall customer experience. This
involves updating the customer regularly with the whereabouts of their package and
ensuring that there are no errors or delays while shipping or transporting the product to
their location.
<!-- p7 -->
Reverse Logistics
• Returns Management
Approximately one-third of all online purchases result in returns to e-commerce
companies. The reverse logistics process comes into action when a customer or a
delivery agent initiates a return request. In contrast, brick-and-mortar stores
experience lower return rates, primarily because customers have the opportunity
to physically inspect products before making a purchase. However, it's important
to note that both models employ distinct logistical procedures to ultimately
achieve their objectives.
<!-- p8 -->
Reverse Logistics
• Value Recovery from Returned Goods
This approach allows you to extract value from returned items through various
means, including selling them in secondary markets, recycling or upcycling them,
donating them to charitable organizations, and converting them into energy
sources. This ensures that every returned product can be repurposed to generate
some level of value, rather than sitting idle and eventually becoming obsolete.
• Eco-Friendly Disposal of Unusable Items
For products that cannot be salvaged or resold, it is essential to dispose of them in
an environmentally responsible manner. This entails sustainable disposal practices
for all types of items, ranging from food, clothing, and electronics to health and
hygiene products.

Key Components of E-Commerce Logistics
• Order Processing
Order processing is the initial step where customer orders are received,
verified, and prepared for fulfillment.
- Involves order validation, payment processing, and inventory allocation.
- Accurate and efficient order processing is fundamental for timely deliveries.
- Order management systems play a vital role in streamlining this process.
<!-- p10 -->
Key Components of E-Commerce Logistics
• Warehousing
Warehousing in e-commerce logistics involves the storage and management of
products prior to shipment.
- Warehouses are strategically located to minimize shipping times.
- Efficient warehousing includes inventory management, product labeling,
and quality control.
- Technology like barcoding and automated storage systems can enhance
warehouse operations.
<!-- p11 -->
Key Components of E-Commerce Logistics
• Transportation and Shipping
Transportation and shipping encompass the movement of products from the
warehouse to the customer's location.
- Various transportation methods, including ground, air, and sea, are utilized.
- Shipping options range from standard to expedited, influencing delivery
times and costs.
- Sustainable shipping practices are gaining importance to reduce the
environmental impact.
<!-- p12 -->
Key Components of E-Commerce Logistics
• Last-Mile Delivery
Last-mile delivery is the final leg of the delivery journey, taking products from
a local hub to the customer's doorstep.
- Last-mile delivery is often the most expensive and challenging part of the
logistics process.
- Innovations like delivery drones, autonomous vehicles, and local pickup
points are reshaping this phase.
- Customer experience is greatly impacted by the quality and speed of last-
mile delivery.

Types of logistics management
• Third-Party Logistics (3PL) Management
3PL refers to third-party companies that offer a range of logistics and supply
chain services.
- Services can include transportation, warehousing, order fulfillment, and
more.
- staff (and their robotic support) in the fulfillment centers pick, pack, and
ship your products to your customers. It’s a time- and cost-saving way to
take the logistical nightmare out of selling online.
- The only difference is that your products are stored, labeled, and packed in a
third-party distribution hub and fulfillment center.
<!-- p14 -->
Types of logistics management
• In-house logistics
- Companies with brick-and-mortar stores or warehouses may choose to
manage their logistics in-house.
- involves the internal handling of the entire supply chain process, from goods
reception to order fulfillment
- In this case, you have a dedicated team that welcomes incoming goods,
stores them, and picks them out when an online order is made. You’ll need
to partner with a shipping carrier—like FedEx, USPS, or DHL—that collects
the orders and delivers them to a customer.
<!-- p15 -->
Types of logistics management
• Dropshipping
- Dropshipping is a logistics model used by many e-commerce retailers.
Unlike in-house logistics, dropshipping involves minimal physical handling of products.
- Retailers don't handle or store the products they sell online.
- When an online order is placed, it's immediately forwarded to a third-party provider
(supplier).
Third-Party Provider Responsibilities
- 1. Picking: The third-party provider picks the ordered items from their inventory.
- 2. Packing: They pack the items and prepare them for shipment.
- 3. Shipping: The provider ships the order directly to the customer.
