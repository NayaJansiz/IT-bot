What is SWOT analysis?
• SWOT analysis is a strategic planning tool used to evaluate businesses Strengths,
Weaknesses, Opportunities and Threats.
• It is a comprehensive framework that helps organizations identify internal and external
factors that may impact their current or future objectives.
1. Strengths: These are the internal positive attributes and characteristics that give an
organization a competitive advantage. Strengths can include things like a strong brand
reputation, skilled workforce, advanced technology, or efficient processes.
2. Weaknesses: These are internal factors that may hinder an organization's performance or
put it at a disadvantage compared to others. Weaknesses could include a lack of resources,
outdated technology, or poor management practices.
3. Opportunities: External factors that an organization can capitalize on to achieve its goals.
Opportunities can arise from changes in the market, new technologies, or emerging trends.
Identifying and exploiting opportunities can help organizations gain a competitive edge.
4. Threats: External factors that may pose a challenge or risk to the organization. Threats can
come from competition, economic downturns, regulatory changes, or other external forces.
Recognizing and addressing threats is crucial for strategic planning and risk management.

What is PESTEL analysis?
• PESTEL analysis, also known as PESTLE analysis, is a strategic
management tool that helps organizations understand and evaluate
the external macro-environmental factors that can impact their
operations.
• The acronym PESTEL stands for Political, Economic, Social,
Technological, Environmental, and Legal factors.
• PESTEL analysis is a valuable tool for strategic planning and risk
management. By considering these external factors, organizations
can anticipate potential challenges and opportunities.
• PESTEL analysis is often used in combination with other strategic
planning tools, such as SWOT analysis, to form a more
comprehensive understanding of an organization's situation.

System Analysis
To get started, answer the question:
What do we want this e-commerce website to do for our business?
Therefore, system functions and information requirements must be defined, and therefore
business objectives are just the privileges you want your site to have
System functions are the information systems you will need to achieve your business goals.
Information requirements for any system are the information elements that the system must
produce in order to achieve business objectives. You will need to provide these lists to your
system developers and programmers.
These objectives must be translated into a description of the system's functions and
ultimately into a set of precise information requirements.
The business goals of an e-commerce website are no different from those of a regular retail
store.
The real difference lies in the system functions and information requirements. In an e-
commerce website, the entire business objectives must be made available in digital form
without buildings or sales representatives, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.

Use Prebuilt Templates
This approach involves using prebuilt templates or themes provided by website-
building platforms like Shopify, WordPress, or Google Sites. These platforms offer a
range of templates that you can customize to fit your needs.
• Pros:
• Quick and easy setup with ready-made designs.
• No need for extensive coding skills.
• Often includes built-in features and plugins.
• Cons:
• Limited customization compared to building from scratch.
• Your website may look similar to other sites using the same template.
• Examples: Shopify themes, WordPress themes,Google Sites templates.

Building a Website Individually (advantages &
Disadvantages)
Building a website from scratch involves writing custom code using programming languages
such as HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and possibly a server-side language like PHP or Python. This
method provides complete control over design and functionality.
• The decision to create your own website involves a number of risks.
With the complexity of features such as shopping carts, credit card authentication and
processing, inventory management and order processing, the costs involved are high.
Risks of doing a poor job.You will be reinventing what other specialized companies have
already done.
The team may face a long and difficult learning curve, delaying entry into the market.
• On the positive side, being better able to create a site that works exactly as intended and,
most importantly, developing in-house knowledge to allow the site to be changed quickly if
necessary due to a changing business environment.

Testing
• Once the system is built and programmed, it must go into a testing process.
• Depending on the size of the system, this may be somewhat difficult and lengthy.
• Testing is required whether the system is outsourced or built in-house. A complex e-commerce
website can have thousands of paths through the site, each of which must be documented and
then tested.
It is important to note that testing is generally under budget. Up to 50% of the budget can be
consumed by testing and refactoring (usually depending on the quality of the initial design).
• Unit testing involves testing software modules on site one by one.
• System testing involves testing the site as a whole, in the same way that a normal user does
when using the site.
• Since there is no truly "typical" user, system testing requires testing every conceivable path.
• Final acceptance testing requires that the company's key employees and managers in
marketing, production, sales, and general management physically use the system as it is
installed on an Internet or intranet test server. This acceptance testing verifies that the
business objectives of the system as originally envisioned actually work

A/B Testing
• Another form of testing is called A/B testing or split testing. This type of testing involves showing
the A and B versions of a web page or website to different users to see which one performs
better.
• There are several different types of A/B tests that can be used for a website design project.
Template testing compares the same general page content using two different layouts and/or
design treatments.
The new concept test compares the control page to a completely different page.
Conversion testing compares the flow through a series of pages (such as the product page, to the
registration page, to the shopping cart page, versus skipping the registration page) to see which
one results in a higher percentage of conversions.
• Multivariate testing is a more sophisticated form of testing than A/B testing. Multivariate testing
involves identifying specific elements or variables on a web page, such as title, image, button,
and text, creating versions of each element, and then creating a unique combination of each
element and version to test.
• For example, if there are three items and two versions of each, there will be eight possible
combinations to test (2 * 2 * 2 = 8).
When used correctly, multivariate testing enables designers to determine the most optimal
layout, color, content, and layout.

IMPLEMENTATION AND MAINTENANCE
• Systems break down for many reasons - most of which are unpredictable.Therefore, they
need constant review, testing, and repair.
• Very large e-commerce sites will see some economies of scale; for example, a $1 million
site will likely require a maintenance budget of between $500,000 and $700,000.
• E-commerce websites are always in the process of change, improvement and correction.
Traditional systems maintenance studies have found that 20% of the time is dedicated to
debugging code and responding to emergency situations (e.g. a new server was installed by
your ISP, all hypertext links are lost and a script is broken!).
• The other 20% of the time is concerned with changes to reports, data files, and links to
back-end databases.
• The remaining 60% of maintenance time is allocated to general administration (making
product and price changes in the catalog) and making changes and improvements to the
system.
• E-commerce websites are never finished: they are always in the process of building and
rebuilding. It's dynamic
